What data, and what stays private

What kind of data are we talking about?

Mostly records of how your business works: SOPs and playbooks, process documents, project and task histories, CRM stages, templates, knowledge base articles and reports. Buyers are interested in how work gets done and how decisions get made. You decide what is included, and you can leave anything out.

Will our clients' or patients' personal information be shared?

It shouldn't be. Agreements in this market require customer names, contact details and other personal or confidential information to be removed before the data is used. You agree in writing how that happens before anything moves. Ask the buyer to explain their anonymization process in writing, and have your lawyer confirm it meets your obligations.

Why don't buyers need our customers' details?

They're interested in how work moves between people and software: a request comes in, someone replies, updates the CRM, then closes it out in billing. That's what AI agents learn from. Who the customer was doesn't matter to them, which is why identities can be removed without losing what makes the data useful.

How does anonymization actually work?

There are two common methods. Redaction blanks out identifying details: "Anna emailed Bob about Acme Corp" becomes "[name] emailed [name] about [company]." Transformation swaps them for made-up ones: "Sean emailed Ed about ABC Company." The workflow stays intact, but the real people and companies are gone. Agree in writing which method is used, who does it, and whether it happens before the data leaves your company.

How long does the buyer keep our data?

It varies by buyer. Some use a one-time export for a set training period, often around 90 days, then delete it. Don't rely on a typical practice: put the retention period and deletion in the agreement, and ask for written confirmation when it's deleted.

Does Alor Solutions or its partners ever see our data?

No. We never ask for, receive or store your business datasets. To start, we only need a general description of the kinds of records you keep. If you sign an agreement, data goes straight from your company to the AI company licensing it.

How is the data transferred and protected?

Only after you sign, and only straight to the AI company licensing it. Security terms belong in the agreement. Ask how the data is encrypted in transit and at rest, who can access it, how long it is kept, and how it is deleted at the end.

What about our team's chats and emails?

Internal messages often contain employee personal information. If you include them, they need the same removal of personal details, and you may need to tell staff or get consent depending on where you operate. Many companies simply exclude them.

Ownership and control

Are we selling our data?

No. This is a license, not a sale. You keep ownership of your records. The license spells out what the buyer may use, for what purpose and for how long.

What is the buyer allowed to do with it?

Only what the agreement permits, usually training and improving AI models. Check whether the buyer may share or resell the data, whether the license is exclusive, how long it lasts, and what happens when it ends.

Could this help a competitor or expose our methods?

It's a fair concern. Models learn general patterns, not your files, but you should still keep out anything you consider a trade secret or a core competitive method. You choose the scope, and you can negotiate limits on how the data is used.

Can we take it back later?

You can usually stop future use under the agreement's termination terms, and ask that copies be deleted. A model that has already learned from the data generally can't unlearn it, so treat what you include as a permanent decision.

Legal and compliance

Do we need our own lawyer?

Yes. Have your own legal adviser review the buyer and the agreement before you sign. We help you prepare the right questions, but we don't give legal advice.

We're a law firm, clinic or accounting practice. Can we take part?

These fields are among the strongest fits, and they carry extra rules: client confidentiality and privilege for law firms, health privacy rules such as HIPAA for healthcare, and professional secrecy for accountants. Your counsel decides which records can be included and how.

Do our client contracts allow this?

Check them. Engagement letters, NDAs, privacy policies and data processing agreements can limit how you use information about clients. Records that are purely internal, such as your own SOPs and templates, are usually simpler.
